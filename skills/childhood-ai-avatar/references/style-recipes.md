# Style Recipes

Apply one recipe to an entire expression set, or use recipes 1–9 in order for a nine-style sampler. Preserve the identity hierarchy in every prompt.

## 1. Clean studio avatar

`soft semi-real portrait, pure warm-white background, floating head-and-shoulders crop, diffused studio key light, delicate natural skin texture, polished but not airbrushed`

Keep the result photographic enough to retain facial proportions. Use this when replicating short-video “AI childhood avatar” examples unless another material is obvious.

## 2. Plush character

`handmade plush-doll interpretation, short soft fibers, embroidered micro-details, rounded forms, warm cream background, soft product-photography light`

Translate features into fabric construction without replacing the face with a generic mascot.

## 3. Clay miniature

`small handcrafted polymer-clay portrait, subtle fingerprints and matte clay texture, rounded but recognizable features, pastel studio backdrop, soft shadow`

Avoid over-rounding the eyes or cheeks when it reduces identity.

## 4. Clean anime illustration

`refined contemporary anime portrait, controlled linework, soft cel shading, natural facial proportions, restrained eye enlargement, clean pale background`

Keep feature placement and hairstyle silhouette from the identity reference; avoid copying a known franchise or living artist's signature style.

## 5. Watercolor storybook

`gentle watercolor-and-gouache children's-book portrait, visible paper grain, translucent washes, precise facial landmarks, airy negative space`

Use controlled edges around eyes, nose, and mouth so the subject remains recognizable.

## 6. Retro children's studio photo

`late-20th-century children's studio portrait atmosphere, soft film grain, muted warm palette, simple period-neutral backdrop, gentle frontal flash softened by diffusion`

Do not change the subject's apparent era-specific identity cues unless the user asks for a period transformation.

## 7. Sticker emoji

`high-quality die-cut portrait sticker, crisp silhouette, narrow white border, bright but controlled colors, smooth simplified shading, transparent or pure-white outer background`

Keep simplification moderate; do not turn the face into a generic emoji.

## 8. Blind-box figurine

`premium collectible blind-box figurine portrait, painted vinyl material, subtle satin finish, compact toy proportions, clean product-photo lighting, pale neutral background`

Preserve the facial landmark pattern and hairstyle silhouette despite the toy proportions.

## 9. Layered papercraft

`layered cut-paper portrait, tactile paper fibers, carefully stacked facial shapes, soft dimensional shadows, restrained pastel palette, clean gallery background`

Use enough layers to keep the eyes, nose, mouth, and face outline individually recognizable.

## Style-sampler controls

- Keep pose, crop, expression, background brightness, and clothing family as stable as each medium allows.
- Use one neutral expression across all nine styles unless the user explicitly asks for mixed expressions.
- Avoid style changes that alter apparent age, facial proportions, or identity-critical hair shape.
- Do not imitate a named living artist. Convert such requests into descriptive visual characteristics.
